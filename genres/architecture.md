# Genre: architecture, interiors, real estate, hospitality

## Honesty first
For a real property, use **real photos** as first frames and animate only light, curtains, water, foliage and a slow camera. Never add features the property does not have (pool, view, extra rooms, renovated kitchen). A fully generated house or room is for concept or brand content only and must be labelled as such. Remind the user to disclose virtual staging or AI enhancement where local rules require it.

## Look
"ceiling-height windows, diffused daylight, oak or travertine floors, minimal furniture, straight vertical lines, 24 mm architectural framing, clean neutral or warm golden grade." Wide shots, 40–60% negative space, no people (or distant silhouettes), slow linear camera moves.

## Hold-mode recipe (per photo)
"The reference photo is the opening frame; preserve the room layout, furniture, windows and doors exactly. Only light and air move: sunlight slides slowly across the floor, the sheer curtain drifts once. Camera: slow linear push-in toward the window. Sound: soft room ambience, distant birds. No new objects, no text; final frame holds for one second."

## Shot sequence (9:16, ~5 s each)
exterior push-in (golden hour) → entry / foyer → living room lateral track → kitchen detail (macro on stone or wood) → primary bedroom → bathroom detail → backyard or view (crane up) → closing wide with space for title.

Zero-cost option: `assemble.py stills` gives every real photo a slow push-in — 100% faithful to the property. Mix it with AI hold-mode shots, or use it alone when budget is tight.

## Avoid
Orbits and fast pans indoors (they bend walls) · ultra-wide distortion · people walking through rooms · generated signage · night shots from daytime photos unless clearly a creative concept.

## Sound
Soft ambience (birds, distant city, fireplace crackle) or a light piano/acoustic bed; no drums. Voiceover optional: 2.5 words per second maximum.
